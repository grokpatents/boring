# High-Speed TBM Cutterhead with Integrated Vacuum Preconditioning for Intercity Tunnels

## Abstract
A tunnel boring machine cutterhead incorporates vacuum ports and segmented pressure seals to precondition soil ahead of the face, reducing effective density by 15-25 percent. This allows sustained advance rates of 45 meters per day in mixed soils between Austin and San Antonio while maintaining 200 mph vehicle capability in the finished tube.

## Problem
Current tunnel boring machines advance at 15-25 meters per day in Central Texas clay and limestone, extending an 80-mile intercity tunnel construction beyond practical timelines and increasing costs. Regulatory and land issues compound delays when advance rates remain low.

## Prior art
- CN106080618B, Ultrahigh speed pipeline passenger and freight transportation system, describes vehicle air compression discharge but does not precondition soil during boring.
- JP2020533231A, Door system for vacuum tube trains, covers tube sealing and propulsion guidance without cutterhead modifications.
- ES2925373T3, Transport system, addresses capsule propulsion on track but assumes finished tunnel infrastructure.

## Summary of the invention
The invention integrates vacuum ports into the cutterhead face to lower interstitial pressure in advance soil, combined with real-time density sensors that adjust cutter torque and thrust. This increases advance rate without enlarging machine diameter or power.

## Claims
1. A tunnel boring machine cutterhead comprising a rotating disk (12) of 14 meter diameter with 48 vacuum ports (14) of 80 mm diameter spaced at 1.2 meter intervals, each port connected to a 0.3 bar vacuum source, and three soil density sensors (18) mounted flush on the face.
2. The cutterhead of claim 1 further comprising segmented elastomeric seals (22) around each port that maintain vacuum differential while allowing 5 mm axial compliance.
3. The cutterhead of claim 1 wherein the vacuum source activates only when sensor (18) readings exceed 2.1 g/cm³, limiting power draw to 180 kW.
4. A method of boring comprising rotating the cutterhead at 4.5 rpm, applying thrust of 120 MN, and preconditioning soil to 0.75 bar absolute pressure for 8 seconds per 0.3 meter advance increment.
5. The method of claim 4 further comprising monitoring torque variance below 8 percent to detect seal failure and automatically isolate the affected port group.
6. The cutterhead of claim 1 wherein ports are arranged in three concentric rings with the outermost ring offset by 15 degrees to prevent soil bridging.

## Detailed description
The cutterhead disk (12) is fabricated from 80 mm thick quenched-and-tempered steel plate with yield strength 950 MPa. Forty-eight vacuum ports (14) penetrate the disk on a 1.2 m grid. Each port (14) connects via 100 mm stainless tubing to a central manifold maintained at 0.3 bar absolute by two 90 kW liquid-ring vacuum pumps. Segmented seals (22) of 70 Shore A EPDM surround each port opening; each segment measures 40 mm wide by 12 mm thick and is bonded to a 3 mm steel backing ring that floats on four 8 mm compression springs. Three soil density sensors (18) of gamma-ray backscatter type are recessed 2 mm below the cutting plane and read at 10 Hz. When density exceeds 2.1 g/cm³ the control system opens the port valves for 8 seconds while advance continues at 0.0375 m per cutter revolution. Torque is measured at the main bearing; variance above 8 percent triggers isolation of the nearest port group via solenoid valves rated 10^6 cycles. In mixed clay-limestone the preconditioned zone reduces required thrust by 18 percent, yielding 45 m/day advance at 4.5 rpm cutter speed and 120 MN total thrust. Failure mode of seal wear is mitigated by the 5 mm compliance range and automatic port isolation, keeping advance rate loss below 6 percent. All dimensions are nominal with ±0.5 mm tolerance on port spacing and ±2 percent on vacuum level.
